#### [A] Solid-state formation of H$_2$CS and CH$_3$SH in molecular clouds: Carbon-initiated reactions with atomic and molecular hydrogen
- **Score:** 76.4  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, molecular_clouds, ism_methods  
- **Authors:** F. Kruczkiewicz, J. C. Santos, G. Fedoseev, S. Ioppolo, C. M. R. Rocha, K.-J. Chuang  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24508)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24508.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24508.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** The solid-state formation pathways of thioformaldehyde (H$_2$CS) and methanethiol (CH$_3$SH) in dense molecular clouds remain poorly constrained. We investigate carbon-initiated reactions in hydrogen sulfide (H$_2$S) ice in the presence of atomic or molecular hydrogen. We co-deposited H$_2$S, ground-state atomic carbon, and H or H$_2$ at 10 K under ultra-high vacuum. We monitored the reaction products using reflection absorption infrared spectroscopy (RAIRS) and characterized their thermal desorption using temperature-programmed desorption (TPD) with quadrupole mass spectrometry. Gas-phase ab initio calculations were used to assess pathways to CH$_3$SH formation. Atomic carbon reacted with H$_2$S at 10 K to form H$_2$CS, detected as both monomers and H$_2$CS-derived oligomers. With atomic H, successive H addition to H$_2$CS leads to CH$_3$SH. With molecular H$_2$, CH$_3$SH also forms efficiently. The results support an alternative pathway in which C reacts with H$_2$ to form CH$_2$, which abstracts H from H$_2$S. The resulting CH$_3$ and SH radicals can then recombine to form CH$_3$SH. The infrared bands and TPD fragmentation patterns support the formation of H$_2$CS and CH$_3$SH in the ice at 10 K. These findings expand the set of viable solid-state pathways to sulfur-bearing molecules and constrain models of sulfur chemistry in prestellar environments.

#### [A] JCMT POL-2 observations of NGC 253: evidence for large-scale reordering of magnetic fields in giant molecular cloud complexes
- **Score:** 76.4  
- **Topics:** magnetic_fields, astrochemistry, molecular_clouds  
- **Authors:** Kate Pattle, Walter K. Gear  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24584)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24584.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24584.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** In this paper we present observations of the magnetic field of the starburst galaxy NGC 253 made in polarized 850 $\mu$m dust emission using the POL-2 polarimeter on the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope (JCMT). While the magnetic field typically traces the galactic spiral structure, we see significant deviations in field direction associated with the presence of stellar and/or supernova feedback, with the mean plane-of-sky magnetic field direction offset 24$^{\circ}$ from the galactic plane. Polarization fraction is anticorrelated with total 850 $\mu$m intensity, with a power-law index of 0.72$\pm$0.03. Using the Fasano-Franceschini test, we find that the positions of POL-2 detections and radio sources in the spiral arms are inconsistent with one another, indicative of depolarization resulting from magnetic fields being tangled on scales smaller than the 240 pc JCMT 850 $\mu$m beam in sites of star formation and feedback. Moreover, in both the starburst superwind and in one star-forming clump in the spiral arms, we observe a magnetic field direction consistent with being directed out of the plane of the galaxy. We thus hypothesise that magnetic fields in the giant molecular clouds (GMCs) of NGC 253 are undergoing an evolutionary sequence from a relatively ordered field parallel to the spiral arms in the pre-star-formation gas, to a complex field caused by the interplay of turbulence, gravity and small-scale feedback in relatively young GMCs, to a field that is reordered out of the plane of the galaxy as large-scale HII region expansion reshapes the gas and dust of galactic spiral arms.

#### [B] Probing the Turbulent Magnetised Interstellar Medium of the Small Magellanic Cloud through Depolarisation with POSSUM Observations
- **Score:** 68.4  
- **Topics:** magnetic_fields, galactic_ism, astrochemistry  
- **Authors:** Jason M. Price, Yik Ki Ma, Amit Seta, N. M. McClure-Griffiths, B. M. Gaensler, S. Lyla Jung, Cameron L. Van Eck, David McConnell, Lindsey Oberhelman, J. D. Livingston, Takuya Akahori, Shane P. O'Sullivan, Denis Leahy, C. S. Anderson  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24121)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24121.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24121.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** The interstellar medium (ISM) of the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) provides a unique laboratory for studying magnetised gas in a low-metallicity, tidally disturbed environment. We investigate this magnetised turbulent medium using broad-band polarimetric data from the Australian Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder (ASKAP)'s Polarisation Sky Survey of the Universe's Magnetism (POSSUM), leveraging a high-density Rotation Measure (RM) grid comprising 951 RMs ($\sim24$ RMs per square degree) to probe the magneto-ionic structure of the SMC. We statistically compare the number densities of polarised and non-polarised non-thermal background sources on a per-square-degree basis, revealing spatially coherent depolarisation signatures that correlate with enhanced plane-of-sky RM fluctuations. This is consistent with a turbulent cascade from large-scale structures. Analysed through RM synthesis and Stokes $QU$-fitting, we attribute the depolarisation to a turbulent magneto-ionic medium within the telescope's beam ($\lesssim6~\mathrm{pc}$). Faraday complexity is detected in $\sim$24% of the RM sources; however, no coherent spatial structure is observed in the complexity distribution, and we attribute this primarily to internal source structure or an extended halo surrounding the SMC rather than to its main body. Furthermore, as previously identified in the Large Magellanic Cloud, depolarisation in the SMC increases with emission measure; however, the underlying Faraday dispersion is $\sim$6 times weaker. With the thermal electron density smooth on the scales of the field fluctuations, this depolarisation is likely driven by magnetic field fluctuations.

- **Authors:** Rebecca K. Leane, Gowri Sundaresan  
- **Subjects:** hep-ph; astro-ph.CO; astro-ph.GA; astro-ph.HE  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23385)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23385.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23385.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** We introduce interstellar optical line emission as a new probe of axion dark matter. Axion decays to photons or electrons can excite or ionize neutral hydrogen, producing H$\alpha$ emission at a fixed atomic wavelength rather than at an energy set by the axion mass. Developing the formalism and applying it to the Leo T galaxy with Very Large Telescope data, we derive strong constraints for eV--GeV scale axions, covering new regions of parameter space for both axion-photon and axion-electron couplings. These results establish optical line measurements as a new discovery channel for axion dark matter, probe poorly constrained parameter space below hydrogen's ionization threshold, and constrain axion explanations of JWST's high-redshift supermassive-black-hole observations.

#### [B] The infrared arc around $α$ Crucis: a weak interaction with a structured interstellar medium
- **Score:** 64.2  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, massive_star_formation, atomic_ism  
- **Authors:** Naira Azatyan, Elena Nikoghosyan, Marine Torosyan, Haik Harutyunian, Derenik Andreasyan, Vardges Grigoryan, Arman Khachatryan, Anahit Samsonyan  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA; astro-ph.SR  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24612)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24612.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24612.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Infrared arc-shaped structures around early-type stars are commonly interpreted as stellar wind bow shocks, although their morphology may also be shaped by gas motions and density variations in the surrounding interstellar medium. We present a multiwavelength study of the infrared arc associated with the nearby $\alpha$ Crucis system, comprising HD 108248 (B0.5 IV), HD 108249 (B1 V), and HD 108250 (B4 V). We combine WISE, Herschel, Planck, stellar astrometry, and eROSITA X-ray data to investigate the structure and its relation to the stellar components. The WISE W4 ridge extends over $\sim$0.65 pc and has a median PSF-corrected width of $\sim$0.037 pc. The HD 108248/HD 108249 pair lies $\sim$0.13 pc from the ridge, compared with $\sim$0.17 pc for HD 108250. Relative to the mean motion of the Lower Centaurus-Crux (LCC) subgroup of the Scorpius-Centaurus association, the close pair is consistent with LCC, whereas HD 108250 has a transverse peculiar velocity of only $\sim$1.8 km s$^{-1}$. Its motion lies within $\sim$23$^\circ$ of the geometrical symmetry axis of the infrared arc, but points in the opposite sense from the arc. Herschel maps show slightly higher median dust temperature ($\Delta T_d\sim$0.8 K) and molecular-hydrogen column density ($\Delta N_{\rm H2}\sim$7$\times$10$^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$) toward the W4-defined region than in the nearby background. On larger scales, Planck maps reveal a strongly structured microwave environment around the system. Both the HD 108248/HD 108249 pair and HD 108250 are detected by eROSITA, with the former approximately 60 times more luminous in X-rays. The combined morphology, kinematics, and dust properties do not support an unambiguous classical high-velocity bow-shock interpretation. Instead, the arc likely traces a weak interaction between the multiple stellar system and an inhomogeneous ambient medium.

- **Abstract:** We present the direct dynamical measurement of the supermassive black hole (SMBH) mass in NGC 3862 (3C 264), a massive Fanaroff-Riley type I radio galaxy. Using ALMA $^{12}$CO(2-1) observations, we map the cold molecular gas kinematics in the circumnuclear disk and model them with the KinMS forward-modeling code. The $^{12}$CO(2-1) disk exhibits a nearly face-on, ring-like morphology with regular disk rotation and a steep central velocity rise, providing clean dynamical constraints on the central gravitational potential. Our best-fit model yields $M_{\rm BH} = 2.40^{+0.52}_{-0.32}[{\rm stat.}] \pm 0.91 [{\rm sys.}] \times 10^9~M_\odot$ and a stellar mass-to-light ratio of $M/L_{\rm F814W} = 4.11^{+0.99}_{-0.27}[{\rm stat.}] \pm 0.17 [{\rm sys.}]~M_\odot/L_\odot$ (both at $1\sigma$ confidence level). This dynamically derived $M/L_{\rm F814W}$ consistent with an old, metal-rich stellar population and a Salpeter IMF. The SMBH sphere of influence is spatially resolved, confirming the robustness of our measurement. This places NGC 3862 among SMBHs with direct molecular gas dynamical constraints, contributing to the census needed to calibrate SMBH-host galaxy co-evolution models at the high-mass end of the scaling relations.
