#### [A] Gradients of Turbulent Fluctuations as New Tracers of Astrophysical Magnetic Fields
- **Score:** 79.3  
- **Topics:** turbulence, magnetic_fields, astrochemistry  
- **Authors:** Yue Hu  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18980)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18980.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18980.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence is ubiquitous in astrophysical plasmas and produces scale-dependent anisotropy with respect to the local magnetic field. Because turbulence cascades preferentially perpendicular to the field, its spatial gradients acquire preferred orientations that can be used to probe magnetic-field structure. This property underlies a family of gradient techniques based on spectroscopic, synchrotron-intensity, and synchrotron-polarization observations. In this review, we summarize the theoretical foundation of these techniques, including the anisotropy of MHD turbulence and the corresponding behavior of velocity, magnetic-field, and density fluctuations. We then describe how these fluctuations are encoded in spectroscopic data and synchrotron observables, and review the implementation of Velocity Centroid Gradients, Velocity Channel Gradients, Intensity Gradients, Synchrotron Intensity Gradients, and Synchrotron Polarization Gradients. We also discuss the effects of isotropic fast modes, shocks, self-gravity, stellar outflows, thermal broadening, and self-absorption. Finally, we review applications to the diffuse interstellar medium, star-forming regions, the Galactic center, external galaxies, and galaxy clusters.

#### [A] Kinetic Monte Carlo modeling of ammonium formate NH$_4^+$HCOO$^-$ and ammonium cyanate NH$_4^+$OCN$^-$ in interstellar ices
- **Score:** 76.1  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, molecular_clouds, turbulence  
- **Authors:** Joseph Salaris, Robin Toonen, Rens Waters, Herma Cuppen  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21433)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21433.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21433.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** JWST observations have revealed evidence for NH$_4^+$ in the ices of the Chamaeleon I dark molecular cloud, with HCOO$^-$ and OCN$^-$ likely counter ions. Laboratory experiments have demonstrated that ammonium formate and ammonium cyanate can form through acid-base reactions involving NH$_3$ at cryogenic temperatures. The formation of these two ammonium salts are simulated to investigate their formation in dark-cloud ices and assess their sensitivity to uncertainties in the chemical reaction network, including the recently considered HOCO-mediated pathway for CO$_2$ formation, as well as variations in the cosmic-ray ionization rate, cloud collapse timescale and density, and elemental depletion. Our modeling framework consists of microscopic kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC) simulations of grain-surface chemistry augmented by UCLCHEM gas-phase and physical cloud evolution simulations. The microscopic KMC method provides for explicit simulation of the location, movement and reaction of ice species, making it particularly well-suited to model acid-base chemistry of NH$_4^+$-salt formation, occurring within the ice mantle over extended timescales. The simulations produce the canonical ice-evolution sequence, with an initially H$_2$O-rich ice followed by a layer dominated by CO and CO$_2$. Salt production is dominated by NH$_4^+$HCOO$^-$, which is found to form predominantly during the H$_2$O-rich stage as its production is limited by the availability of NH$_3$. NH$_4^+$OCN$^-$ is found to form later at the interface between the H$_2$O- and CO-rich ice when the formation of its precursor acid HNCO is enabled. Despite the variations explored, the total NH$_4^+$ abundance, particularly relative to H$_2$O, remains remarkably robust and consistent with the observed abundance. Depending on the variation, a redistribution between counterions HCOO$^-$ and OCN$^-$ is observed.

#### [A] OGHReS: Discovery of a giant hub-filament system at the edge of the Galactic molecular disc
- **Score:** 74.6  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, molecular_clouds, galactic_ism  
- **Authors:** J. S. Urquhart, A. Y. Yang, M. A. Thompson, C. Koenig, D. Colombo, W.-J. Kim, A. Giannetti, A. Karska, F. Wyrowski, M. Figueira, T. J. T. Moore, L. K. Morgan, Enrique Vázquez-Semadeni  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21400)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21400.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21400.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** We present the discovery and characterisation of G195.65$-$0.10 (also known as the $\lambda$-complex), a giant hub-filament system identified by the Outer Galaxy High Resolution Survey (OGHReS). The complex contains 61 spatially and kinematically coherent HiGAL clumps with a mean velocity of $V_{\rm LSR}=32.85$ km/s and a velocity dispersion between clumps of 1.97 km/s. A $Gaia$ parallax measurement of the central emission-line star MWC 137 associated with an embedded cluster gives a heliocentric distance of 5.2$^{+0.8}_{-0.6}$ kpc, placing the complex at a Galactocentric radius of 13.2 kpc. The molecular structure spans approximately $35\times63$ pc and contains $\sim 1.6\times10^{5}$ M$_\odot$ of molecular gas, including $\sim 5 \times 10^{4}$ M$_\odot$ in dense clumps. The molecular emission is organised into three prominent velocity-coherent filaments that converge on a dense central hub. Of the associated clumps, 16 are protostellar and 45 are gravitationally bound but currently starless. The protostellar clumps are significantly more concentrated towards the hub than the starless population, with median projected offsets of 6.65 and 15.83 pc, respectively. Moreover, the luminosity-to-mass ratio of the protostellar clumps decreases significantly with increasing distance from the hub, indicating that the most evolutionarily advanced sources are preferentially located in the central region. These results reveal a systematic concentration of dense gas and increasingly evolved star formation towards the hub, with organised large-scale gas motions throughout the filamentary network. The $\lambda$-complex therefore provides an unprecedented laboratory to understand hierarchical and high-mass star formation in the outer Galactic disc.

- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA; astro-ph.SR  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20967)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20967.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20967.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** To investigate the processes governing clustered star formation, we present a multi-wavelength study of the star-forming complex hosting clusters Waterloo 01 and [BDS2003]64 in Sh 2-208. Both clusters exhibit elongated morphologies, with radii of $\sim1.5$ and $\sim0.9$ pc, respectively, and are located at a common distance of $\sim3.5$ kpc. Wat01 displays hierarchical structure ($Q=0.6$) and signature of mass segregation ($\Gamma_{\rm MSR}=1.5\pm0.6$). Its relaxation time, comparable to or longer than its age, suggests that the observed segregation may have a primordial contribution. The mass function follows a single power law, $\Gamma=-1.62\pm0.15$, over $\sim0.7$-$19.6$ M$_\odot$. Molecular-line observations reveal distinct velocity components associated with S208 and Wat01, indicating different molecular environments within the complex. Wat01 is strongly shaped by massive-star feedback, with an evacuated cavity, surrounding shell, and compressed globules consistent with an expanding H\,{\sc ii} region; feedback may have promoted localized star formation, although a direct causal connection cannot be established. In contrast, \textit{Herschel} data uncover a hub-filament system (HFS) in S208, with the central hub associated with BDS64 and the H\,{\sc ii} region powered by 'S1'. The filaments remain relatively well-preserved, indicating that gravity continues to play an important role in assembling dense gas toward the hub and in clustered star formation, even as feedback begins to affect the immediate surroundings. Overall, this complex illustrates the coexistence of feedback-processed and comparatively less-disrupted star-forming environments, highlighting the evolving interplay between stellar feedback and gravity in clustered star formation.

#### [B] Inferring accretion bursts in intermediate-mass protostars from asteroseismic imprints
- **Score:** 59.4  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, ism_methods, massive_star_formation  
- **Authors:** Johannes Jørgensen, Eduard Vorobyov, Konstanze Zwintz, Ebraheem Farag, Adolfo Carvalho, Jan Henneco  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.SR  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20154)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20154.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20154.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** During star formation, objects may gain the majority of their final mass through regular and intense bursts of mass accretion. For bursts of the FU Orionis-type (FUor), accretion rates can climb to $10^{-3} \ \text{M}_\odot \text{yr}^{-1}$ during short windows of time, amounting to just a few percent of the main accretion phase. To study the occurrence of such events observationally, signatures of the current accretion bursts and tracers of recent bursts are crucial. We investigate the potential to identify accretion bursts in protostars and pre-main sequence stars of intermediate mass using asteroseismology. We use the stellar structure and evolution code MESA to evolve stellar models from the protostellar seed phase of $0.01 \ \text{M}_\odot$ and $1.5 \ \text{R}_\odot$ up to $4.0 \ \text{M}_\odot$ with a constant accretion rate of $10^{-6} \ \text{M}_\odot \text{yr}^{-1}$. A single accretion burst is initialized during the accretion phase. The stellar oscillation code GYRE is employed to compute the resulting asteroseismic properties of the models. Accretion bursts of up to $10^{-3} \ \text{M}_\odot \text{yr}^{-1}$ are sufficient to cause the convective cores of the models to recede, leaving behind a chemical trace which manifests itself in the form of quasi-periodic dips in the g-mode period spacing. This effect is strongest during the outburst, but also lingers for up to 475 years before the convective core is re-established and the dips are gone. Our experiment shows that a single accretion burst leaves an imprint in the deep interiors of protostars and pre-MS stars of intermediate mass, and that this imprint can be captured with asteroseismology. Our results are most conclusive for burst magnitudes greater than $1\times10^{-4} \ \text{M}_\odot \text{yr}^{-1}$, corresponding to FUor-type bursts.
