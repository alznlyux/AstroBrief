- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21142)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21142.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21142.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Galactic observations have shown that N2H+ intensities preferentially trace star-forming dense molecular gas compared with HCN or HCO+, which are conventionally used as dense-gas tracers in extragalactic studies. In this study, we aim to verify this trend in the nearby star-forming galaxy M83. Using ALMA, we observed the J=1-0 transitions of N2H+ and HCN, along with the 1-mm and 3-mm continuum, and analyzed the archival CO(1-0) data. The data achieved a spatial resolution of 48 pc in the southwestern bar-end region of M83, covering an area of 2.1 kpc x 1.8 kpc. N2H+ and HCN intensities are approximately linearly correlated, indicating that HCN luminosity can still reasonably predict the amount of dense gas as well as N2H+ luminosity does. However, in regions with high column densities, N2H+ becomes a super-linear function of HCN, suggesting that the N2H+ emission is more sensitive to dense gas than HCN. We employed a radiative transfer model that predicts line intensities as a function of column density, adopting parametric column density probability distribution functions (N-PDFs) to describe the underlying column density distribution. The model demonstrates that the observed linear or superlinear trend can be explained by a log-normal distribution only or a log-normal and a power-law tail distribution in the N-PDF. Furthermore, the N2H+ intensity is significantly more sensitive to the power-law tail than the HCN intensity. In addition, our analysis incorporating archival data shows that N2H+ is a less reliable tracer of dense gas in galactic center regions, where warmer temperatures may promote N2H+ destruction.
