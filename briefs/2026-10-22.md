- **Abstract:** Recent molecular line observations with high spatial resolutions have revealed vertical chemical stratification in Class I/II disks (i.e. chemical composition changing with height in the vertical direction of the disk), as well as a radial variation of ionisation rates. However, such chemical layered structures in Class 0 disk and envelope systems have been investigated only for limited objects, such as L1527. In the context of the ALMA Large Program FAUST, we report the results of molecular line observations toward the disk and envelope around the low-mass Class 0 protostar NGC 1333 IRAS 4C in the Perseus Molecular Cloud. Previous observations have shown that the disk rotation axis of this object is largely tilted to the line of sight, making it suitable for studying the molecular abundance distribution as a function of disk height. We found that the molecules known as gas density tracers such as C18O and H2CO are distributed around the disk midplane. - **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28746.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28746.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** We perform a multiple velocity component analysis of ammonia (NH$_3$) gas from KEYSTONE, a Green Bank Telescope survey covering 11 giant molecular clouds (GMCs) at distances from 0.7 to 2.7 kpc. We extend existing codes to enable simultaneous fitting of up to three velocity components in NH$_3$ (1,1) with radiative transfer. Using our multiple component fitting technique, we produce the best maps of the centroid velocity and velocity dispersion in these clouds to date. Over the entire survey, multiple velocity components were seen in 13% of pixels where we detect and fit NH$_3$ emission, primarily in clusters at the heart of the GMCs. In these multiple component pixels, the median measured NH$_3$ linewidth decreased by a factor of 1.4 compared to equivalent single-component measurements, but there are also significant cloud-to-cloud variations. Regions with multiple velocity components tend to be found in the densest zones of the GMCs and at the sites of high-mass star formation, indicative of multiple filaments, cores, or complex velocities associated with the formation of massive stars. Disentangling these velocity features will allow future quantitative analysis of coherent motions, such as infall and outflow, in these complex environments. The multi-component data products are available on the CADC.

#### [B] Arepo-CD: Coupling Collisional $N$-Body Dynamics with Moving-Mesh Magnetohydrodynamics
- **Score:** 74.9  
- **Topics:** massive_star_formation, turbulence, halo_cgm  
- **Authors:** Chuizheng Kong, Hui Li, Long Wang, Yunwei Deng, Elizabeth Moreno-Hilario  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA; astro-ph.IM  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28727)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28727.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28727.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Star formation is a hierarchical, clustered process in turbulent giant molecular clouds (GMCs), where close stellar encounters can strongly affect cluster evolution and feedback. However, gravitational softening in many hydrodynamic simulations of GMCs limits the treatment of these encounters. We present Arepo-CD (Collisional Dynamics), a framework that couples star-by-star collisional $N$-body dynamics to the moving-mesh magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) code Arepo. Star-star interactions use unsoftened gravity, while interactions involving gas or collisionless components remain softened. Compact stellar subsystems are identified on the fly and evolved with a fourth-order forward symplectic integrator and slow-down algorithmic regularization coupled to the native Arepo timestep hierarchy. Benchmark tests against the collisional $N$-body code PeTar show good agreement in cluster core collapse and subsequent expansion, radial density profiles, and stellar velocity distributions. Coupled to the Rigel star formation and feedback model, Arepo-CD follows cluster formation and early evolution in radiation MHD simulations of a $10^5~M_\odot$ turbulent molecular cloud. In the fiducial GMC comparison, the collisional and softened-gravity runs yield similar star formation histories and integrated star formation efficiencies. Nevertheless, close gravitational encounters during the embedded phase generate a substantial runaway population, with a velocity tail extending beyond 100 km/s. Ejected massive stars redistribute radiative feedback into the surrounding lower-density medium and produce a more extended distribution of heated gas. Arepo-CD provides a practical and computationally efficient framework for studying collisional stellar dynamics and its coupling to gas evolution and feedback during star cluster formation in realistic galactic environments.

- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28717)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28717.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28717.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** The relative abundances of chemical elements in the interstellar medium (ISM) trace the history of star formation and chemical enrichment in galaxies. We investigate the abundance patterns of carbon (C), nitrogen (N), neon (Ne), sulfur (S), and argon (Ar) in star-forming galaxies at z=1-7, combining ultra-deep JWST/NIRSpec observations from the MARTA survey with archival spectroscopy. We assemble 244 galaxies with [OIII]4363 detections and derive direct Te-based abundances. New empirical strong-line calibrations for N/O, Ne/O, S/O, and Ar/O extend the analysis to an additional 514 galaxies. We find distinct abundance-metallicity trends. N/O is enhanced by 0.18 dex in median relative to local galaxies, with a stronger enhancement at low metallicity. Direct measurements reveal decreasing S/O and Ar/O with increasing metallicity, while strong-line measurements suggest a flattening at higher metallicities, especially for S/O. We also identify a stellar-mass dependence in the O/Ar-Ar/H plane, with lower-mass galaxies exhibiting lower O/Ar at fixed Ar/H, consistent with differences in star-formation efficiency and delayed SNe Ia enrichment. Ne/O remains approximately constant up to 12+log(O/H)=8.2, with a mild increase at higher metallicities and no significant redshift evolution. C/O tentatively increases with metallicity, broadly following the local relation. Finally, a forward model shows that observational selection affects the abundance ratios differently: it mainly restricts the accessible metallicity range for Ne/O and Ar/O, but preferentially selects galaxies with enhanced N/O at low metallicity, with a weaker effect for S/O. These results provide new constraints on chemical enrichment at high redshift and highlight the importance of accounting for selection effects when interpreting abundance-metallicity trends.

- **Abstract:** We present the spatially resolved star formation history (SFH) of the interacting, low-mass, gas-rich galaxy pair NGC 4485/NGC 4490, based on deep JWST/NIRCam imaging obtained as part of the FEAST program. Using resolved stars' near-infrared color-magnitude diagrams (CMDs) and the SFERA 2.0 CMD fitting code, we reconstruct the SFH of the system, including the tidal bridge connecting the two galaxies. We find that recent star formation is widespread across the system, extending beyond the main bodies of the galaxies into the tidal bridge and other off-center areas. In particular, a significant fraction of the recent star formation in NGC 4485 occurs outside its central region. All major regions of the system exhibit two prominent episodes of enhanced star formation, occurring around 0.5-1 Gyr and 10-30 Myr ago, with the tidal bridge showing the largest increase relative to its average star formation rate. These peaks in star formation activity occur a few hundred million years after the epochs of the first and second pericenter passages predicted by independent dynamical models, supporting a scenario in which repeated tidal interactions triggered the observed bursts through gas redistribution and compression. Our results place direct observational constraints on the formation history of NGC 4485/NGC 4490 and provide evidence for the important role of galaxy interactions in driving spatially extended, bursty star formation in low-mass galaxies.
