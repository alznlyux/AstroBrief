#### [A] Molecular signatures of shocked gas and enhanced cosmic-ray ionization in supernova remnant W49B revealed by ALMA
- **Score:** 75.6  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, molecular_clouds, turbulence  
- **Authors:** Wenjin Yang, Ping Zhou, Zhi-Yu Zhang, Gao-Yuan Zhang, Bon-Chul Koo, Yan Gong, Ruo-Yu Liu, Hidetoshi Sano, Tian-Yu Tu, Yang Chen, Tram N. Le, Lianghao Lin, Friedrich Wyrowski  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13519)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13519.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13519.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Galactic supernova remnants (SNRs) interacting with molecular clouds (MCs) provide important laboratories for investigating how supernova feedback is transferred to the interstellar medium. We performed observations of the HCO+ J=1-0, 3-2, and 4-3 lines, as well as the CO J=3-2 line, toward the eastern and western regions of SNR W49B using the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) and the Atacama Pathfinder Experiment (APEX) telescope. In W49B west, we identify twelve regions with broad-line profiles in both HCO+ and CO emission, with FWHM of 25-60 km/s, providing clear evidence of SNR-MC interaction. These emissions are compact (at $\sim$5 arcsec resolution) and spatially coincident with 2.12~$\mu$m H$_2$ emission, but generally offset from the 1.64~$\mu$m [Fe~II] and radio continuum peaks. Non-LTE analyses yield shocked-gas densities of (0.5-1.6) $\times 10^5$~cm$^{-3}$ and high $N_{\rm HCO+}/N_{\rm CO}$ abundance ratios of (4.2-14.5) $\times 10^{-4}$. Reproducing such high abundance ratios in shocked MCs requires high cosmic ray ionization rates (CRIRs) of a few 10$^{-14}$-10$^{-13}$~s$^{-1}$, about 3-4 orders of magnitude above the standard Galactic level. Some regions exhibit enhanced HCO+/CO intensity ratios in their high-velocity line wings, possibly due to multiple unresolved shocks spanning a range of velocities and gas densities, spatial variations in the CRIR, or both. By contrast, the ratio remains nearly constant with velocity in other regions. For these regions, we further explore the propagation of low-energy CRs through the post-shock layer. An energy-budget analysis of a representative shocked clump indicates that the shock-associated kinetic-energy reservoir substantially exceeds cumulative CR and X-ray heating inputs, with CR heating exceeding X-ray heating.[abridged]

#### [B] Revisiting the centrifugal breakout scaling relationship for H$α$ emission in light of $ρ$ Oph C, the coolest B-type star with an H$α$-bright centrifugal magnetosphere
- **Score:** 65.6  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, magnetic_fields, molecular_clouds  
- **Authors:** M. E. Shultz, E. Alecian, I. Berry, J. Krtička, J. Labadie-Bartz, P. Leto, S. P. Owocki, A. ud-Doula, G. A. Wade  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.SR  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14452)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14452.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14452.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** The corotating magnetospheres of B-type stars are strongly affected by magnetic field strength and rotational velocity, which determine both the geometry and strength of visible and radio emission; however, H$\alpha$ emission largely disappears below a bolometric luminosity of $\log{(L / L_\odot)} \sim 2.8$. The nearby B5 V star $\rho$ Oph C (HD 147932) is the least-luminous rapidly rotating magnetic B-type star to display magnetospheric H$\alpha$ emission. While $\rho$ Oph C's radio magnetosphere has been extensively studied, its magnetic field and H$\alpha$ magnetosphere have been constrained only via snapshot observations. We present a new dataset of high-resolution ESPaDOnS spectropolarimetry covering the star's rotational period, which is revisited by combining Hipparcos, K2 and TESS photometry. We find that the star's magnetic field strength and geometry are consistent with the model inferred from radio data. As expected from the magnetic model, there is very little variability in H$\alpha$. Comparison of the light curve to both equivalent width measurements from metallic lines and magnetospheric models suggests that the star's photometric variability is dominated by surface chemical abundance spots; however, there is complex rotationally modulated variability at about the mmag level which probably originates in the magnetosphere. $\rho$ Oph C is the least luminous B-type star in which magnetospheric H$\alpha$ emission has yet been detected, and its emission is notably weaker than the predictions of the centrifugal breakout model. Its photometric eclipse depth is also much lower than predicted by the CBO model. However, its radio luminosity is consistent with CBO predictions. We speculate on this basis that $\rho$ Oph C may be a transitional object between effective temperature regimes with hydrogen-dominated and decoupled metallic winds.

#### [B] Angular momentum transport in a newborn protostar--disc system: magnetic braking, spiral arms and the magneto-rotational instability
- **Score:** 60.5  
- **Topics:** magnetic_fields, turbulence, astrochemistry  
- **Authors:** Antonin Borderies, Benoît Commerçon, Adnan Ali Ahmad  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.SR; astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13729)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13729.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13729.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Angular momentum transport in young protostar--disc systems involves magnetic braking, magneto-rotational turbulence and gravitational instability, mechanisms that are usually studied separately and on different scales. We present a three-dimensional non-ideal magnetohydrodynamic simulation, including Ohmic and ambipolar diffusion, that follows the collapse of a $1~M_\odot$ dense core continuously from the cloud scale to the formation of the protostar, and then the resulting protostar--disc system for $\sim 64~\mathrm{yr}$ at a resolution of $\sim 10^{-4}~\mathrm{AU}$ at the protostellar surface. We decompose the azimuthally averaged angular momentum budget into Reynolds, Maxwell and gravitational torques, each split into a radial and a meridional component, over a quasi-steady phase of the evolution. The collapse produces a thick disc an order of magnitude more massive than the protostar, whose specific angular momentum decreases as it accretes. Accretion through the disc is vertically layered, with accreting and decreting layers at the same radius, a pattern set layer by layer by the torques. The transport separates into three regimes delimited by the coupling of the magnetic field to the gas. Magnetic braking operates in the envelope. Maxwell stresses dominate inside $\sim 0.1~\mathrm{AU}$, where the field recouples and the magneto-rotational instability develops. In the ambipolar dead zone that makes up the bulk of the disc, the Reynolds stress dominates, carried by $m=2$ spiral arms in a marginally unstable disc, with the gravitational stress as a secondary contributor. Meridional transport is as important as radial transport, so that a one-dimensional viscous model fitted to the net mass flux would require a negative $\alpha$ over part of the disc.

#### [B] Dwarf galaxy simulations with stellar-core mass- and metallicity-dependent core-collapse supernova explosion energies
- **Score:** 58.7  
- **Topics:** massive_star_formation, astrochemistry, halo_cgm  
- **Authors:** Natalia Lahén, Kiril Maltsev, Thorsten Naab, Hans-Thomas Janka  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14437)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14437.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14437.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Core-collapse supernovae (CCSNe) are a critical source of thermal and kinetic energy and chemically enriched material in galaxies. Leveraging recent advances in CCSN modelling, we implement a novel core-mass- and metallicity-dependent prescription for stellar explodability and CCSN explosion energies, $E_\mathrm{expl}$, in the SPHGal galaxy evolution code. It accounts for black hole (BH) formation via SN failures and via partial fallback of ejecta mass, and adopts an explosion-progenitor connection calibrated to three-dimensional CCSN simulations, such that $E_\mathrm{expl}$ varies between $<0.1$-$2\, \mathrm{B}$. We ran simulations of isolated low-metallicity galaxies with variations in the input stellar feedback models. With the new explodability prescription nearly three times more BHs form compared to the canonical CCSN-model, but the reduced number of successful CCSNe has minimal impact on galactic star formation owing to the low fraction of failed CCSNe. While BH formation contributes to removal of matter from the galactic baryon cycle, the latter remains driven by galactic outflows, and most compact remnant mass is stored in white dwarves. The most significant change is instead brought about by the new average-$E_\mathrm{expl}$ that amounts to $\sim33$\% of the $1\,\mathrm{B}=10^{51}\, \mathrm{erg}$ traditionally assumed in sub-resolution CCSN-models. This reduction results in increased and less bursty star formation, reduced outflows and mass-loading factors, increased star cluster formation efficiency, and enhanced chemical enrichment and metal-recycling in star-forming regions. These results stand in contrast with outflow-launching and baryon cycle modelling in lower-resolution galaxy simulations in which canonical (i.e., $1\, \mathrm{B}$) CCSN feedback is typically assumed to produce galaxy models that match observed scaling relations.
