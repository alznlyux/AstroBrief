#### [A] Magellanic Outflow and Chemistry Survey (MAGOS): the Outflow-Luminosity Scaling Relations in the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds
- **Score:** 68.5  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, massive_star_formation, halo_cgm  
- **Authors:** Suinan Zhang, Kei E. I. Tanaka, Yichen Zhang, Takashi Shimonishi, Yu Cheng, Kenji Furuya, Asako Sato  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16830)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16830.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16830.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Understanding massive star formation in low-metallicity environments is crucial for constraining the universality of star formation across cosmic evolution. In this Letter, we present the first systematic survey of massive protostellar outflows in the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds (LMC and SMC, $Z\sim$0.2--0.5 $Z_\odot$), as part of the Magellanic Outflow and Chemistry Survey (MAGOS). MAGOS targets 40 massive young stellar objects (MYSOs) with luminosities of (1--23)$\times 10^4$ $L_\odot$ with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) at 0.85 mm at a linear resolution of $\sim$0.1 pc. Using CO (3--2), we identify blue- and red-shifted outflows associated with the primary continuum sources toward 29 targets in the LMC and 10 targets in the SMC. Comparing the outflow mass, momentum, energy, and force versus luminosity scaling relations in MAGOS with those in the Galaxy, we find that the LMC CO outflows have a similar slope in the mass-luminosity relation, suggesting that CO remains an effective tracer of massive protostellar outflows at $Z\sim$0.5 $Z_\odot$. In contrast, despite the large scatter, the SMC CO outflows are deficient at high luminosity and show flatter scaling relations than the LMC and the Galaxy. This is consistent with reduced CO abundances due to enhanced photodissociation at high luminosity and low metallicity ($Z\sim$0.2 $Z_\odot$), although the possibility of intrinsically weak outflow launching cannot be ruled out with the current data.

#### [A] How Intensity Threshold Shapes the Velocity Dispersion-Size Relation in Molecular Clouds
- **Score:** 68.0  
- **Topics:** turbulence, molecular_clouds, astrochemistry  
- **Authors:** Yaru Qi, Xin Liu, Fujun Du  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14852)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14852.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14852.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** The velocity dispersion-size relation of molecular clouds, $\sigma_v = C\,(R/1\,{\rm pc})^{\gamma}$, has been reported with a power-law index ranging from $\gamma \approx$ 0.38 to 0.5 or higher, and a correspondingly varying normalization coefficient $C$, but the role of the intensity threshold in producing this scatter has not been quantitatively characterized. Using 4916 $^{13}$CO clouds identified with GDCluster (Liu & Du 2025) in the Milky Way Imaging Scroll Painting (MWISP) survey, we derive $\gamma$ and $C$ as functions of the intensity threshold $T_{\rm th}$. The index $\gamma$ increases monotonically with threshold, reaching a saturation value $\gamma_{\rm sat} \approx$ 0.56 for the full sample; splitting the sample by spiral arm gives higher values, $\gamma_{\rm sat} \approx$ 0.62 and 0.70. The normalization $C(T_{\rm th})$ declines monotonically, saturating at $C_{\rm sat} \approx$ 0.35 km/s and implying a surface density of ~46 Msun/pc^2 under a virial approximation. On the $\gamma(T_{\rm th})$ curve, $\gamma \approx$ 0.38 (Larson 1981) and $\gamma \approx$ 0.5 (Solomon et al. 1987) correspond to a low threshold ($T_{\rm th} \approx$ 1.2 K) and a near-saturation one (2.6 K), respectively, roughly consistent with the $^{13}$CO-equivalent boundary temperatures of these two studies. These two historically debated values, together with the broader $\gamma \approx$ 0.38-0.7 range reported in the literature, may largely reflect different positions along this single threshold-dependent curve and differences in sample composition. This threshold effect should therefore be considered when using Larson-type relations to assess the roles of turbulence and gravity. As an independent check, DBSCAN-identified $^{13}$CO and $^{12}$CO samples show the same monotonic trend in $\gamma$, though the curve shape differs quantitatively.

- **Abstract:** Recent high-angular-resolution observations of protostellar outflows have revealed complex spatial and velocity structures that are difficult to describe with a single shell. We have investigated the primary outflow of the Class 0 protostar IRAS 15398$-$3359 in the H$_2$CO and CS emission observed with ALMA and identified four shell-like components, which trace the cavity walls of the more extended CO outflows. Non-LTE radiative-transfer calculations of H$_2$CO and synthetic ALMA observations of the three-dimensional model show that a common ellipsoidal geometrical form and Hubble-law-like velocity field can broadly reproduce the channel-map and position--velocity structures of the identified shells when the geometry, spatial configuration, and kinematics of each shell are allowed to vary independently. - **Abstract:** We present new radio-continuum observations of the well-known Wolf-Rayet (WR) nebula PCG 11 (Cohen's Ring; named after and in honour of the co-author on the discovery paper, Martin Cohen) using observations from the Australian Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder (ASKAP) Evolutionary Map of the Universe (EMU), Australia Telescope Compact Array (ATCA) and MeerKAT surveys. - **Abstract:** The Magellanic Stream (MS) is a gaseous structure extending over $150^\circ$ across the sky, likely formed through interactions between the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds (LMC and SMC) as they fell into the Milky Way (MW). #### [B] Cosmic Ray Reacceleration and Gamma-Ray Emission via the Cloud-Wind Interaction in the Galactic Halo
- **Score:** 67.9  
- **Topics:** turbulence, halo_cgm, molecular_clouds  
- **Authors:** Ryohei Inamoto, Jiro Shimoda, Katsuaki Asano  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.HE  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15120)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15120.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15120.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** The interaction between cosmic rays (CRs) and multiphase gas in the Galactic halo may play an important role in shaping the hard gamma-ray spectrum of the Fermi bubbles. We investigate the interaction between a hot Galactic wind and a cold, dense gas clump with a relative velocity of $300\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ using two-dimensional hydrodynamic simulations coupled with momentum-dependent CR transport. The system produces a bow shock and wind reacceleration region downstream, where the wind flow is faster than the initial velocity. For a CR pressure comparable to the gas pressure (CR-rich), the wind acceleration and turbulence behind the clump are enhanced. When CR pressure is dynamically weak, strong gas compression leads to significant CR spectral hardening. In contrast, for the CR-rich case, CR pressure gradients weaken the gas compression and suppress further CR reacceleration. We estimate the gamma-ray intensity from CRs, and find that reaccelerated CRs penetrate the dense clump and enhance hadronic gamma-ray emission. These results suggest that an ensemble of dense gas clumps interacting with the Galactic wind may contribute to the gamma-ray emission of the Fermi bubbles.

- **Authors:** Sadhana Singh, Thiem Hoang, Pham Ngoc Diep, Nguyen Bich Ngoc, Woojin Kwon

- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16385)

- **Abstract:** We investigate magnetic fields ($B$-field) and grain alignment mechanisms in Serpens Main using 850 $\mu$m dust polarization observations from JCMT/POL-2. We derive a map of $B$-field strength and calculate the mass-to-flux ratio and Alfvénic Mach number, suggesting the subdominance of $B$-fields over gravity but their dominance over turbulence. Fitting the Ricean mean model to the nondebiased polarization fraction ($P$) as a function of emission intensity ($I$) yields a slope $\alpha = 0.68\pm0.01$, revealing depolarization. To understand its origins, we examine the dependence of $P$ on gas column density ($N({\rm H}_{2})$), dust temperature ($T_{\rm d}$), and polarization angle dispersion function ($S$). We find no correlation between $P$ and $S$, whereas for $P\times S$, an observational proxy for alignment efficiency decreases with increasing $N({\rm H}_{2})$. However, neither $P$ nor $P \times S$ shows a correlation with $\delta_{\rm mag,sp}$, suggesting that enhanced grain alignment due to magnetic relaxation is insufficient to compensate for the reduced alignment associated with increasing $a_{\rm align}$ toward denser regions.

- **Authors:** Nicholas Choustikov, Harley Katz, Taysun Kimm, Matthew J. Hayes, Aaron Smith, Crystal L. Martin, Shyam H. Menon, Larrance Xing, Joki Rosdahl, John Chisholm, Aayush Saxena, Martin P. Rey, Adrianne Slyz, Julien Devriendt, Alex J. Cameron, Anatole Storck, Corentin Cadiou

- **Abstract:** Constraining the Lyman Continuum (LyC) escape fraction ($f_{\rm esc}$) of high-redshift galaxies is essential to connect the abundant populations revealed by \textit{JWST} to cosmic reionization. We investigate this interplay using {\small MEGATRON}, a suite of zoom simulations with detailed radiation/chemistry and varied feedback prescriptions. We find that feedback physics simultaneously regulates UV source counts, LyC escape fractions, and escaping ionizing luminosities ($\dot{N}_{\rm ion,esc}$), producing different reionization histories. These differences are governed by the delay between a starburst and LyC escape: efficient early feedback and lower-density environments enable photons to escape while pre-supernovae stellar populations remain strongly ionizing. Consequently, models that produce fewer UV-bright galaxies yield earlier LyC escape and reionize sooner than models with more abundant UV sources and delayed LyC emission. Furthermore, UV-faint galaxies follow a common physical sequence in ISM structure with increasing $f_{\rm esc}$ irrespective of feedback physics: stronger leakers ($1\%\leq f_{\rm esc}\leq 50\%$) exhibit evidence of strong photoionization and SNe feedback alongside post-starburst star formation histories, while extreme leakers ($f_{\rm esc}\geq 50\%$) have also cleared their cold/dense ISM. We find that electron densities inferred from the $\rm [O\,{II}]\,\lambda\lambda3727$ doublet are a candidate diagnostic of $f_{\rm esc}$, with lower densities around $\rm H~{\small II}$ regions selecting stronger leakers. Moreover, this density can trace the early evolution of $f_{\rm esc}$, whereas UV slope-based calibrations over-predict $\dot{N}_{\rm ion,esc}$ for individual young galaxies by up to two orders of magnitude. Overall, the delayed duty cycle of LyC leakage must be accounted for when UV source counts are interpreted in the context of reionization.
