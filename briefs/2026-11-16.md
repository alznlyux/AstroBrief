#### [A] The ISM-CGM Transition of a Star-forming Dwarf Galaxy Resolved in Ionized Gas Emission
- **Score:** 68.9  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, halo_cgm, massive_star_formation  
- **Authors:** Chloe Neufeld, Pieter van Dokkum, Imad Pasha  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17480)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17480.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17480.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** The circumgalactic media (CGM) of dwarf galaxies are expected to be tightly coupled to their star formation, as low virial temperatures imply a CGM rich in cold accretion and shallow potential wells allow for more effective feedback. In this work, we use Keck/KCWI and KCRM to spatially resolve the ionized gas at the boundary between the interstellar medium (ISM) and CGM in dwarf galaxy UGC 6578 (M* = 10^7.8 Msun), out to ~0.1 Rvir. We discover a clear transition between the ISM and CGM at 0.033 Rvir, where the Halpha surface brightness profile changes from an exponential to a shallow power law, marking the first such detection at M*/Msun < 10^8. BPT diagnostics and ionization budget calculations indicate that the gas in the inner CGM is likely ionized by the galaxy's own star formation activity rather than shocks or the UV background. Spatially resolved emission maps also reveal a kinematically distinct, low-metallicity clump embedded in the inner CGM, and we discuss the possibility that this is a direct detection of ongoing gas accretion. This work serves as a pilot study for the Baryon Recycling in Dwarf Galaxies Emission Survey (BRIDGES), which will combine spatially resolved KCWI observations of the ISM-CGM interface in low-mass galaxies with wide-field observations of diffuse ionized gas out to the virial radius with the MOTHRA array.

#### [B] Observations of stellar feedback: tracers, diagnostics, measurements, and physical interpretation
- **Score:** 68.7  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, molecular_clouds, turbulence  
- **Authors:** Anna F. McLeod  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17592)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17592.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17592.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Stellar feedback is central to galaxy evolution, yet the coupling of radiation, stellar winds, and supernovae to the multi-phase interstellar medium remains uncertain. This review treats this as a problem of inference, framed around four qualitative axes of ignorance: observational diagnostics are indirect; stellar population synthesis models must be ground-truthed before being used to infer feedback quantities; recent high-redshift observations have opened a new regime that may require re-calibration; and stellar feedback and the affected environment are interdependent. We examine the inference chain, from observables to physical quantities and from those quantities to how energy and momentum are transferred to the surrounding medium, across the principal diagnostics of stellar feedback: ionised gas, mechanically-driven bubbles, giant molecular cloud lifetimes, galactic outflows, and Lyman-continuum escape. We ask how much observations reveal about the link between the feedback-driving massive stars and the feedback-driven interstellar medium. We further discuss how stellar population synthesis assumptions, environment (e.g., metallicity, dust content), and galaxy properties (e.g., stellar mass, gas content) affect feedback measurements, and how high-redshift observations are testing calibrations established in the nearby Universe. The emerging picture is that interpreting a feedback measurement requires explicit control of the inference chain and of its physical context; feedback should therefore be contextualised, i.e. interpreted in terms of mechanism, gas phase, environment, and spatial and temporal scale. Progress will depend on measuring feedback-related quantities across the full range of star-forming systems and, more importantly, on obtaining observations (real, and synthetic from post-processed simulations) that match the same tracer and spatial scale across the same systems.

#### [B] Counter-rotating Ionized and Neutral Gas in a Star-forming Elliptical Galaxy
- **Score:** 51.5  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, molecular_clouds, massive_star_formation  
- **Authors:** Pralay Biswas, Yogesh Wadadekar, Omkar Bait, Sushma Kurapati  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17740)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17740.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17740.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** In early-type galaxies, gas that is kinematically misaligned with the stars generally rotates together across all gas phases, implying a single external origin. Using upgraded Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope (uGMRT) 21 cm imaging and SDSS-MaNGA spatially resolved spectroscopy, we studied MaNGA 10214-1902, a compact ($R_e = 0.78$ kpc), star-forming elliptical galaxy with stellar mass $\log M_*/M_{\odot} = 9.90$, in which the ionized gas counter-rotates with respect to the stars. It hosts $\log(M_{\rm HI}/M_{\odot}) = 9.65$ in a 45 kpc HI disk that co-rotates with the stars and has a central column-density depression. Inside this depression, the ionized gas ($r \lesssim 2$ kpc) rotates in the opposite sense to both the stars ($\Delta{\rm PA} = 152^\circ \pm 20^\circ$) and the HI that surrounds it ($\Delta{\rm PA} = 179^\circ \pm 10^\circ$). HI co-spatial with the ionized gas is not detected, so a fainter inner HI component with a different orientation cannot be ruled out. This central gas is star-forming in the [SII] and [OI] diagnostic diagrams and has EW(HI) $>6~\mathrm{\mathring{A}}$ at the center. The 1.4 GHz radio-continuum star formation rate agrees with the spectral energy distribution based value, showing no evidence of a dominant active galactic nucleus. The stellar population at the center is younger than the outskirts and has the lowest D$_{\rm n}$4000. These results are consistent with the acquisition of gas with opposite angular momentum into the center of a pre-existing HI disk that co-rotates with the stars, which can fuel the most recent central star formation.
