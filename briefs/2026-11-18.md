#### [B] The interplay between ionized and warm molecular gas in the turbulent intragroup medium of Stephan's Quintet
- **Score:** 73.3  
- **Topics:** atomic_ism, turbulence, galactic_ism  
- **Authors:** Philip Appleton, Pierre Guillard, Ute Lisenfeld, C. Kevin Xu, Francois Boulanger, Jonathan Braine, Aditya Togi, Sarah Gallagher, Pathayappura Aromal, Bjorn Emonts, Loreto Barcos Munoz, Pierre-Alain Duc, Benjamin Godard, George Helou, Patrick Ogle, Ewan O'Sullivan, William Reach, Tanio Diaz Santos  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22007)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22007.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22007.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Using JWST MIRI spectroscopy, we explore the multi-phase intergalactic gas in the hot halo of the Stephan's Quintet compact group. Unlike the coolest H2 emission, which commonly shows double- peaked profiles corresponding to the velocity of the intruder and the intragroup gas, the ionized Neon emission-lines are often systematically shifted redwards in radial velocity away from the lowest H2 peak, and into the region corresponding to gas decelerated by the collision of the intruder galaxy with intragroup gas. The majority of the brightest clumps of ionized gas are spatially correlated with the brightest H2 clumps, suggesting they are coherent structures caught up in the opposing gas flows. Gas that is progressively heated, is shifted in velocity further from the velocity of the coldest H2 peaks. Larger and larger shifts in velocity are seen as one progresses from cool to warm molecular hydrogen, to lower-excitation (Neon lines) ionized and strongly shocked heated ionized gas (gas-phase Iron). The majority of the ionized Neon in the intergalactic medium is consistent with excitation in low-velocity shocks. We suggest that this behavior is the result of gas of progressively lower densities being shocked and accelerated by the interaction of pre-existing clouds with a hot flow created by the blast-wave from the collision between the intruder and the intragroup gas. One exception is the extragalactic star forming region, SQ-A, which shows higher-excitation ionized emission lines consistent with heating from a young starburst, consistent with previous photometry of young star clusters in the region.

#### [B] A PhEW Good Simulations: I. Galaxy Properties
- **Score:** 68.4  
- **Topics:** halo_cgm, astrochemistry, turbulence  
- **Authors:** Lisiyuan Yang, Neal Katz, Evan Scannapieco, Aditi Vijayan  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21716)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21716.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21716.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Although galactic outflows driven by stellar feedback are widely implemented in modern cosmological simulations, the interactions between them and the circumgalactic medium (CGM) remains poorly resolved. To overcome this limitation, we conduct a suite of cosmological simulations that use the PhEW (Physically Evolved Winds) subgrid model, which analytically tracks the exchange of mass, metals, momentum, and energy by winds with the ambient CGM using an algorithm based on high-resolution cloud-crushing simulations. Our results show that even without AGN feedback, our simulations are able to accurately reproduce the observed galactic stellar mass functions (GSMFs) and stellar mass-halo mass (SMHM) relations at $z\gtrsim 1$, after which the massive galaxies in our simulations start to overproduce stars. Our simulations also match the observed relations of $M_{\mathrm{HI}}-M_*$, $M_{\mathrm{H_2}}-M_*$, $Z_*-M_*$ and $Z_{\mathrm{ISM}}-M_*$. Taken together these results suggest that AGN feedback is much less important for galaxy formation and evolution than previously believed. However, like found in many previous simulations, the specific star formation rates (sSFRs) of our galaxies are significantly lower than those observed at $z\gtrsim1$ at all stellar masses. Significantly, we find that our results are robust against changes in the resolution and volume of our simulations, without the need to retune our wind parameters.

#### [B] Shocks and Trails in the Circumgalactic Medium Driven by Runaway Supermassive Black Holes
- **Score:** 63.5  
- **Topics:** halo_cgm, turbulence, galactic_ism  
- **Authors:** Kung-Yi Su, Claude-André Faucher-Giguère  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA; astro-ph.HE  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21738)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21738.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21738.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** Runaway supermassive black holes (SMBHs) have long been predicted. RBH-1, discovered with HST and confirmed by JWST, provides the strongest evidence yet for a runaway SMBH: a bow shock and linear trail in the circumgalactic medium (CGM) of a $z=0.96$ galaxy. The bow shock is $\sim$kpc wide, the trail is $\sim 60$ kpc long, and the SMBH apparently moves at $\sim 1,000$ km s$^{-1}$. Using controlled hydrodynamic simulations, we show that the SMBH's gravity alone cannot produce a significant bow shock, but a comoving wind source can sustain one similar to RBH-1's. The wind must either be active or have shut off less than $\sim 3$ Myr ago. The required recent wind momentum flux is $6\times10^{32}\,{\rm dyn}\,(n_{\rm CGM}/10^{-3}\,{\rm cm}^{-3})$, where $n_{\rm CGM}$ is the CGM density. Assuming the wind reaches peak strength only during the last $\sim 6$ Myr, the minimum integrated wind mass is $3.5\times10^5\,M_\odot\,(n_{\rm CGM}/10^{-3}\,{\rm cm}^{-3})$. A $2\times10^7\,M_\odot$ SMBH accreting at $1\%$ Eddington could power the wind. Supernova feedback from a star cluster carried by the SMBH is an alternative, although the required mass and momentum fluxes $\gtrsim 60$ Myr after ejection are difficult to reconcile with a standard initial mass function. For a hot ambient CGM at $\sim 10^6$ K, the quasi-linear trail constrains the 1D velocity dispersion to $\sigma_{\rm 1D}\lesssim 30$ km s$^{-1}$, above which it becomes strongly disturbed. Runaway SMBH trails therefore offer a new probe of turbulence in the diffuse CGM, in regimes where its kinematics are otherwise inaccessible.

#### [B] The Role of Galactic Shear in Regulating Local Star Formation Efficiency: A Combined MaNGA DynPop and ALMaQUEST Study
- **Score:** 59.3  
- **Topics:** halo_cgm, galactic_ism, massive_star_formation  
- **Authors:** Kai Zhu, Shude Mao, Michele Cappellari, Sara L. Ellison, Tao Jing, Lihwai Lin, Shengdong Lu, Mallory D. Thorp, Yong Shi, Ran Li, Hsi-An Pan  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22300)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22300.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22300.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** We study the local regulation of star formation efficiency (SFE) in star-forming regions of nearby galaxies using spatially resolved data from the Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory (MaNGA) and the ALMA-MaNGA QUEnching and STar formation (ALMaQUEST) surveys. We define the local SFE as $\Sigma_{\rm SFE} \equiv \Sigma_{\rm SFR}/\Sigma_{\rm H_2}$, where $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ is the star formation rate surface density derived from dust-corrected $\rm H\alpha$ emission, and $\Sigma_{\rm H_2}$ is the molecular hydrogen surface density derived from CO measurements. Combining these data with dynamical properties from the MaNGA DynPop project, we use random forest regression to identify the dominant factors of $\Sigma_{\rm SFE}$. Galactic shear, quantified by the Oort parameter $A_{\rm Oort}$, emerges as the dynamical property most strongly associated with lower SFE on average, exceeding the predictive power of mass surface density (either total or stellar), Toomre stability, and mid-plane pressure. However, these quantities provide only modest predictive power and primarily trace the radial variation of SFE. After removing the shear-related radial trends, only weak correlations remain between SFE and other local galactic properties, implying that the remaining azimuthal variations are likely governed by cloud-scale processes below our resolution limit. Furthermore, the relationship between SFE and shear varies among galaxies and weakens in gas-rich regions, suggesting that gas density may modulate the impact of shear. This behavior is consistent with competition between shear-induced cloud stabilization and shear-driven cloud-cloud collisions. Overall, galactic shear appears to regulate the large-scale radial distribution of SFE, while cloud-scale interstellar medium physics may drive the remaining variation.

#### [B] A Catalog of Young Stellar Objects in the Lagoon Nebula
- **Score:** 58.4  
- **Topics:** astrochemistry, molecular_clouds, massive_star_formation  
- **Authors:** L. M. Rebull, L. Venuti, A. M. Cody  
- **Subjects:** astro-ph.SR; astro-ph.GA  
- **arXiv:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21962)  
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21962.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21962.pdf)  
- **Abstract:** M8, the Lagoon Nebula, has been known for more than 100 years as a region forming stars. It is (in projection) very close to if not in the Galactic Bulge. As a result, the source surface density is very high and source confusion is a substantial challenge. Because of the location, there have been many surveys that serendipitously observed M8, in addition to studies specifically targeting it. But to this point, there has been no effort to resolve source confusion across all the existing catalogs, spanning many different wavelengths, sensitivities, and spatial resolutions. We have done all of this source reconciliation, and present the merged catalog here. The net result is 3019 YSOs with largely photometric data and/or YSO selection from ~50 different origins, primarily ranging from $g$-band through 25 um, but with a significant number of X-ray sources, and some data of limited sensitivity out through 70-450 um. The catalog includes several flags that enable users to select, e.g., the YSOs that have been identified as young by the most studies, or the YSOs with the most multi-wavelength counterparts, or the ones with the clearest IR excesses. We use this catalog in a companion paper; it will also be of use in, e.g., future SPHEREx and Roman work.
